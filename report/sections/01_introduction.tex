Ski jumping is a competitive winter sport where aerodynamic performance, take-off dynamics, and in-flight posture determine the length and safety of each jump.
Accurate reconstruction of a jumper's trajectory is essential for performance analysis, coaching feedback, and injury prevention. 
This project focuses on developing a robust pipeline to reconstruct two-dimensional trajectories of ski jumpers using only avaible online video recordings.

The main objectives are: to design and implement algorithms for precise spatiotemporal tracking of the athlete during in-run, take-off, flight and landing phases;
 Methodologically, the project explores camera calibration(not for now), multi-view geometry, pose estimation, filtering techniques (e.g. Kalman or particle filters), and optimization-based smoothing.

Expected outcomes include a reproducible implementation for trajectory reconstruction, quantitative performance metrics (accuracy, latency, robustness), and guidelines for practical deployment in training environments. 
The remainder of the report describes related work, the chosen methods and system design, experimental results, and conclusions with future directions.
